Rizal: The Power of Ideas, Unity, and Revolution

Who Was José Rizal?

José Protacio Rizal Mercado y Alonso Realonda was born on June 19, 1861, in Calamba, Laguna. He studied at the Ateneo Municipal de Manila and the University of Santo Tomas, then continued his studies in Spain at the Universidad Central de Madrid, where he earned a degree in medicine. He later trained in ophthalmology in Paris and Heidelberg.

Rizal was a writer, physician, and reformist. His novels Noli Me Tangere (1887) and El Filibusterismo (1891) exposed abuses under Spanish colonial rule. He called for peaceful reform—equal rights, representation, and education—rather than armed rebellion.

His ideas awakened national consciousness among Filipinos, and today he is honored as a national hero of the Philippines.

Topic 1

The Propaganda Movement

In the 1880s and 1890s, educated Filipinos in Spain—known as ilustrados—campaigned for reforms in the Philippines. They asked for representation in the Spanish Cortes, equality before the law, freedom of speech, and the secularization of parishes.

Key figures included José Rizal, Marcelo H. del Pilar, and Graciano López Jaena, the first editor of La Solidaridad. Founded in Barcelona in 1889, the newspaper published essays and reports exposing colonial abuses.

The Propagandists used writing, journalism, and education as their weapons for change.

Key Takeaway: Words and ideas can challenge injustice and prepare a people for change.

Topic 2

La Liga Filipina

On July 3, 1892, Rizal founded La Liga Filipina in Tondo, Manila, after returning from Europe. It aimed to unite Filipinos and work peacefully for reform through collective action.

Major objectives

  • Unite the whole archipelago into one compact body
  • Mutual protection in every want and necessity
  • Defense against violence and injustice
  • Encourage education, agriculture, and commerce
  • Study and apply reforms

Just days later, on July 6, 1892, Rizal was arrested and then exiled to Dapitan. The Liga soon split, and some members—including Andrés Bonifacio—went on to form the Katipunan.

Topic 3

Exile in Dapitan (1892–1896)

Spanish authorities accused Rizal of spreading anti-friar and anti-government ideas and exiled him to Dapitan in Mindanao. Rather than give up, he spent four years serving the community.

📚

Education

Ran a school for local boys, teaching languages, science, and practical skills free of charge.

⚕️

Medicine

Opened a clinic and treated patients, often the poor, without payment.

🌱

Agriculture

Introduced better farming methods and promoted crops for the community.

💧

Engineering

Built a water system to bring clean water to the town.

Lesson for today: Even in difficult circumstances, knowledge used in service of others can transform a community.

Topic 4

The Philippine Revolution

Decades of colonial abuses and a growing sense of Filipino nationalism led to revolution. The Katipunan, a secret society founded by Andrés Bonifacio in 1892, launched the armed revolt in August 1896.

Rizal did not lead the Katipunan or command the Revolution. He believed in peaceful reform and did not support an uprising at that time. Still, his writings had shaped the national awareness that inspired the revolutionaries.

Accused of rebellion, Rizal was executed in Bagumbayan (now Luneta) on December 30, 1896. His death strengthened the resolve of the revolutionary movement.

Reform vs. Revolution: Rizal sought change through ideas and education; the Katipunan pursued independence through armed struggle.

Rizal’s Legacy in the Digital Age

Rizal believed education was the foundation of a free and just society. His commitment to writing, social awareness, and peaceful reform still speaks to us today.

Modern students—especially IT students—can carry this legacy forward by building websites, digital archives, and educational apps that preserve Philippine history, and by using technology responsibly to share truth instead of misinformation.
